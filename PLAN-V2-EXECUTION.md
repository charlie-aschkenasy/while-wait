### Top risks

- Phase 5 is ~71h (5a 20 + 5b 9.5 + 5c 19 + 5d 23) — over half the total — and is strictly ordered 5a->5b->5c->5d with no parallelization; any single game's feel iteration blocks the whole tail. Sudoku (5d, 23h) is the single longest phase and its hard-difficulty generation may exceed ~150ms, forcing background pre-generation (extra scope).
- Trivia-bank generation (~1659 questions to the 2000 target) is the largest non-code effort; if coupled to 1a it dominates calendar. Mitigation: ship 1a with the verified seed now and fill via the parallel track. Season-drift answers (Ovechkin trap) require human spot-check (1a-2).
- Phase 1b puts a buffered hook + synchronous registry writes on the hook/hide path — a real threat to the instant-hide + stateful-resume guarantees (#1/#5). Only a day-long real two-window session (1b-8) can sign this off, adding wall-clock latency after code-complete.
- Open VSX namespace/publisher verification and icon design+approval are calendar-gated and independent of coding; if not started during Phase 1 they inject dead time into Phase 2. Namespace id (charlieaschkenasy vs charlie-aschkenasy) is unresolved.
- Every phase is gated by a human feel/taste handoff (1a-7, 1b-8, 1c-10, 2-10, 3-8, 4-6, 5a-8, 5b-6, 5c-9, 5d-11). These add wall-clock latency between code-complete and phase-done that coding-days do not capture, and each requires an installable .vsix on a real Cursor session.
- Lower-is-better best-time persistence recurs across 5b/5c/5d against the raise-only persist guard (panel.ts:158). The recommended encode-in-webview approach is safe but obligates the Phase 3 Stats tab to decode game-specific keys (e.g. minesweeper ~9899, not seconds) — a cross-phase coupling to lock early.
- extension.ts is touched by 1a, 1b, 1c and Phase 3; if any of these are worked concurrently, merge/coordination friction is likely — keep Phase 1 internal edits serialized.
- 1c-5 first-run notice is the single sanctioned exception to guardrail #6 (no toasts/nudges) and must not land without explicit human sign-off; a wrong call here erodes the product's core no-nudge ethos.
